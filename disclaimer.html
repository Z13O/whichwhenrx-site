Medical Disclaimer

Last updated October 2, 2026

Not medical advice

whichwhenRx helps you organize and remember your medicines and supplements. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and it is not a substitute for your doctor, pharmacist, or other qualified health professional.

Always follow the instructions of your prescriber and the label on your medicine. If anything in the app differs from what your doctor or pharmacist told you, follow your doctor or pharmacist.

Never start, stop, skip, or change a medicine or dose because of something shown in the app without first checking with a health professional.

Interaction and safety checks are not complete

Interaction, duplicate-ingredient, condition, food and timing checks are based on a limited set of rules and on manufacturer label text. They cannot catch every interaction or risk, and the absence of a warning does not mean a combination is safe.

Ask your pharmacist to review everything you take, including supplements and over-the-counter products.

Information may be wrong or out of date

Product details come from public databases (U.S. FDA via openFDA, the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database, Open Food Facts) and retail barcode data. These sources can contain errors, may not match your package, and change over time. Check everything against the label on your own container before saving.

whichwhenRx is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, the National Institutes of Health, or any other organization whose data it displays.

Reminders can fail

Reminders depend on your phone's settings, battery, notifications and software, and may not always arrive. Do not rely on the app as your only way of remembering important medicines.

In an emergency

If you think you or someone else has taken too much of a medicine, has had a serious reaction, or is in danger, call your local emergency number (911 in the U.S.) right away. In the U.S. you can also call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222.
